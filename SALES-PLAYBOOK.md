# SUPERMEGA.dev — Sales Playbook

*Everything needed to actually sell and close. Pairs with [POSITIONING.md](./POSITIONING.md) (the thesis), [MONEY-MACHINE.md](./MONEY-MACHINE.md) (the engine), and the live funnel (supermega.dev → /work/ → /demo/ → /offers/ → /contact/). Payment is collected manually by KBZPay / MMQR / bank transfer **after** the scope call — no online checkout.*

---

## 1. The pitch

**One line (the hook):**
> "We build the exact software your business needs — custom, but at SaaS prices, because we build it with AI. And you own it."

**30 seconds:**
> "Most shops rent five or six SaaS tools that don't talk to each other and get more expensive every time you hire. We do the opposite — we build you one system, shaped to exactly how you work, AI-native, in MMK with KBZPay and MMQR, and it keeps working offline. You pay once and you own it. Because we build with AI, custom now costs about what a year or two of those subscriptions would."

**2 minutes** = the 30-second version + one relevant case from `/work/` ("here's a real one, live, you can open it right now") + the one thing you'd build them first.

**Always end on a live URL.** Open DeskPOS (pos.supermega.dev) or Payslip Maker on your phone in front of them. The demo is the proof.

---

## 2. Who to sell to (ICP, narrow first)

In priority order:
1. **Existing/past clients and warm intros** — zero acquisition cost, already trust you. Start every week here.
2. **Owner-run SMBs feeling the pain:** multi-branch F&B / retail (POS + daily close), distributors (dealer/retailer chaos), small manufacturers (log books + Excel), clinics/salons (bookings + payments), payroll-heavy offices.
3. **Premium-aspiring SMEs** — specialty cafes, boutiques, new D2C brands who want to look and run better.

**Disqualify (politely):** one-person stalls with no recurring pain, anyone who wants "an app like Facebook," tyre-kickers with no budget signal. A custom build needs a real, repeated, money-touching problem.

**Best signals a lead is real:** they already do the work manually every day; they've outgrown a spreadsheet; they've been burned by per-seat SaaS pricing; they mention KBZPay/MMQR reconciliation pain.

## 3. The qualifying call (15 minutes, 6 questions)

1. **What's the one task that eats the most time or causes the most mistakes today?** (the wedge)
2. **Walk me through how it's done now** — what files, sheets, chats, or paper? (find the real, messy source)
3. **Who does it, and who needs to see the result?** (users + the owner's view)
4. **What happens when it goes wrong?** (the money-at-stake — this justifies the price)
5. **What are you using now and what does it cost you per month?** (anchor vs. SaaS TCO)
6. **If this worked, what would you do with the time/money back?** (their win = your case study)

End with: *"I'll send you a short proposal — what we'd build first, the price, and the timeline. If it's a fit, a 50% deposit by KBZPay or MMQR gets us started."*

---

## 4. Pricing (anchor with the tiers, quote on the call)

Lead with the live `/offers/` anchors. USD anchors, MMK at 4,000 MMK/USD.

| Tier | From | Use it when… |
|---|---|---|
| Tool in a week | $600 · 2.4M MMK | One sharp, single-job tool. Great first yes / foot in the door. |
| Custom dashboard / internal tool | $1,500 · 6M MMK | "Our numbers live in five spreadsheets." |
| AI agent / automation | $2,500 · 10M MMK | A recurring task eats hours daily; draft-and-approve fits. |
| Design + ship system (flagship) | $6,000 · 24M MMK | They want it to look premium AND run. The big yes. |
| Care plan | $300/mo · 1.2M MMK/mo | Sold AFTER a build. Hosting + changes + 1 improvement/qtr. |
| Email / Drive Agent | $600 + $200/mo · 2.4M + 800k/mo | Gmail inbox or Drive file → AI action. Fastest foot in door. |
| Digest Agent | $300 + $150/mo · 1.2M + 600k/mo | Daily/weekly AI summary. Easiest build — 1–2 days. |

**Rules:**
- Always **fixed scope + a revision cap** ("two rounds of changes included"). Kills scope creep.
- Quote a **single number**, not a range, once you understand the job. Ranges read as uncertainty.
- If they flinch on price, **shrink the scope, not the price** — drop to "Tool in a week" and land the first yes. Expand later.
- The care plan is the recurring revenue — always offer it at handover, never up front.

## 5. The deposit ask (KBZPay / MMQR — the close)

> "To start, it's a 50% deposit — you can send it by KBZPay or MMQR, whatever's easiest. I'll send the QR / number once you confirm the scope. The balance is due when it's live and you've signed off."

- **50% to start** is the Myanmar norm and protects both sides against ghosting.
- Send the **KBZPay number / MMQR QR privately** after they confirm — never publish account details on the site.
- Confirm receipt, then send a one-line written scope summary ("here's exactly what I'll build, two revision rounds, live by [date]") so both sides are aligned before work starts.
- Balance on **go-live + sign-off**. For care plans, monthly by KBZPay/MMQR.

---

## 6. Objection handling

- **"Too expensive."** → "Compare it to what you'll pay for SaaS over two or three years — and that grows every time you hire. This is paid once and it's yours. We can also start smaller with a single tool for $600 and grow from there."
- **"Why not just buy an existing app?"** → "You can — but you'll bend your business to fit it, pay per seat forever, and stitch it to your other tools yourself. We build the one that fits you, and it's yours to keep."
- **"Can you really build [X]?"** → Don't claim — **show.** Open a live one from `/work/`. "Here's a real system we built and shipped. Yours would be built the same way."
- **"Is my data safe / what about AI?"** → "Anything that sends a message, moves money, or changes your books needs your approval first — the AI drafts, you approve. Your data stays yours."
- **"I need to think about it."** → "Of course. Can I send the short proposal so you've got the scope and price in writing? If it helps, we can start with just the first piece."
- **"What if it breaks?"** → "That's the care plan — hosting, fixes, and a shipped improvement each quarter. Or take it and run it yourself; it's yours."

## 7. Outreach templates (you send these — never auto-sent)

**Cold first message (Viber / Messenger / email), short:**
> Hi [name] — I run SuperMega, we build custom business software for Myanmar shops and factories (in MMK, with KBZPay/MMQR, works offline). Saw [specific thing about them]. We built [relevant live example] — [link]. If [their likely pain] is on your plate, I can show you in 15 minutes what we'd build first. Worth a quick call?

**Warm intro follow-up:**
> Thanks [referrer] for connecting us. [name], here's a live tool we built that's close to your situation: [link]. Free for a 15-min call [day] to see if it's a fit?

**Post-demo follow-up (same day):**
> Great talking, [name]. As promised — here's the live demo again: [link]. I'll send a short proposal tomorrow with what we'd build first, the price, and timeline. Anything you want me to factor in?

**Note:** lead with Telegram/Viber/Messenger (where Myanmar SMBs actually are). Keep it in their language — write the Burmese version for local owners. Never send to lists; every message is to one named person you've researched.

## 9. After the deposit (deliver so they refer you)

1. **Confirm scope in writing** (the one-liner) before building.
2. **Ship the first useful version fast** — a live URL beats a long roadmap.
3. **Hand it over as a running thing**, walk them through it, collect the balance.
4. **Offer the care plan** at handover.
5. **Ask for the referral** (#1 channel in Myanmar) and **ask to write it up** as a `/work/` case study (de-identified if they prefer).
6. **Log it in the graduation tracker** — 3rd time anyone asks for the same thing, it becomes a product.

---

## 10. The one rule

**Never pitch what isn't built as if it's live.** Show real, live things; describe honestly what we'd build. That honesty is the differentiator and it's why referrals come. (See [working-style-feedback].)
